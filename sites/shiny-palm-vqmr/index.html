An independent creator presents

THE LAST LIGHT

“Life always finds a way around.

This time, it found an alien one.”

Logline

In a residential district emptied so suddenly that the cars are still parked and the bins still standing, a single seedling pushes up through a crack in the asphalt — and what grows out of it did not come from Earth.

Synopsis

Nobody agrees on what the morning was. There was no blast, no fire, no ruin. The buildings are intact. The tyres are flat only because time made them flat. Somewhere between one hour and the next, a city of ordinary people simply stopped being a city of people, and the dust began to move across the road unwatched.

Then something grows.

At first it is nothing — a green shoot in a crack, the kind of thing that has been reclaiming abandoned places for as long as there have been abandoned places. It leans toward the light. It puts out leaves. It behaves, in every observable way, like life doing what life does.

And then, without any moment you could point to as the moment, it stops being a plant. The leaves turn translucent. Colour arrives that has no name in any field guide. The roots lift the road like a slow tide, and from somewhere far above, curtains of pale growth begin to descend — reaching, finding the buildings, taking hold.

By the time anything is tall enough to be seen from the sky, the question is no longer what happened to the people. It is what has decided to live here instead, what it is carrying in those slow-pulsing lights, and whether the quiet that follows is an ending or the first patient hour of something else.

The city is not dying. That is the part nobody is left to find comforting.

Stage chain — the six locked frames

The street is generated once and never regenerated. Every ground-level stage below is that same plate with a subject keyed from a flat grey backdrop composited on top at a fixed pixel position — background difference measured at 0.00 grey levels, trunk base pinned to the same pixel throughout. Road damage is a separate layer revealed in proportion to the tree's mass. This is the final chain (rev 7); all earlier revisions are superseded.

Verdict: tier mattered more than model. gpt-image-2 at HIGH is the only run in this test that did everything asked - it resisted the anatomy nouns, held the street photographic, and was the ONLY chain of the four in which the subject genuinely escalated in scale from seedling to canopy as instructed. Its LOW tier had already beaten the others on instruction-following at a fourteenth of the price, but collapsed into decorative pattern at the two most complex frames; HIGH fixed the aerial completely and made the interior usable, so that collapse was a tier artifact, not the model's character. seedream-4.5 gives a fine photographic base plate but is the one model that took the anatomy bait, rendering a human figure as the trunk. nano-banana is the most filmic first plate of all four and cheap, but it edits by addition rather than transformation (it grew a second seedling instead of enlarging the first) and never escalated scale. Practical routing: draft at gpt-image-2 LOW, finish hero frames at HIGH, keep seedream for wide photographic plates with no body nouns anywhere in the prompt. MEDIUM turned out not to be a middle way: at 3x the price of LOW it buys scale escalation and cleaner mid-chain frames, but it reverts to LOW's decorative collapse on the two hardest frames, which is exactly what one would be paying to avoid. The ladder is therefore not linear - LOW for drafts, HIGH for anything that has to survive, and MEDIUM skipped.
